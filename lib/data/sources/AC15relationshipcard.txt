INTRODUCTION TO RELATIONSHIP CARDS
 Within the Cards System there are two types of relationship or interaction formulæ 
(WHY and HOW) which denote the inter-relatedness that is occurring when two, or 
more, symbols come together, not only between people but also for any relevant set of 
Cards; for example, an individual’s Sun-Card to a Day-Card or a Location-Card. This 
first section touches briefly on the main techniques, which are going to be expanded 
following the Tables of Composite Cards. 
 The first of these techniques is called Synastry, which, in Astrological terms, is 
the overlay comparison of two Natal Charts, noting the positions of the various Planets, 
House positions, etc., and finding what falls where in each other’s charts. For the Cards 
System, this primarily becomes the analysis of the type of Links found between two 
Sun-Cards and then their respective Birth Chart Cards, on both the Pure Spread and/or 
the Life Spread, plus this can be extended to each persons’ Age Spreads for current year 
influences. These Links denote the type of inter-relatedness that is occurring when two 
symbols are matched, showing WHY two people react, when in contact, in the way they 
do, but, this does not indicate HOW they are going to work, just that an energy is available for good or bad. Also, another new technique that I call the Dynamic Cards falls into 
this category of relationship studies, which are essentially based on the internal spacing 
between two Cards on the Pure and/or Life Spreads and will be studied later in the text. 
 The second of these techniques is known as Composites – the Adding of two 
or more symbols to find one symbol that helps to explain the relationship. In Astrology, they are simply called Composite Charts, but which are essentially a mid-point chart 
for two people, found by adding together the longitudes (the Sign with its Degree & 
Minute) of any two planets or significant points (both Mercurys, Moons, Mid-Heavens, 
House Cusps, etc.), then dividing the sum by two, creating a new middle position for

each of the points in a chart. However, for the Cards System this is where the Solar 
Values of two or more cards are added to produce one Card Symbol, without any division. The Composite cards denote HOW the energy of the inter-relatedness is going to 
work. Robert Hand, in his Planets in Composite, states in the introduction: “In reading 
composite charts I have found little or none of the ambiguity that occurs with conventional techniques of Synastry.” To a certain point I have to agree with this statement, but, 
the Astrological technique is still only a mid-point system and as such, it implies an 
equality to how a partnership will operate, which is never the case. For the Cards System 
there is no mid-point problem, this technique is the pure composition (addition) of two 
Cards to create a third Card that represents the blending of those energies and how they 
will operate together. The process is easy, one just adds the Solar Values (see page 2) of 
the relevant cards to find the Composite Card. Should the result of your addition exceed 
52, then subtract 52 as many times as necessary in order to get a final number that is 52 or 
less. Once you have the composite number, find the corresponding Composite Card on 
the Solar Values Table. I have set up some examples on the following pages to assist you. 
 The subsequent pages 8 through 15 have a complete set of Tables, for those who 
would prefer not to do the mathematics. Find the relevant card across the top, follow 
the line down to the appropriate horizontal line for the other card you wish to add, and 
there is your Composite Card.

contents of 2 Charts connect with each other, what patterns do they share, how do each

RELATIONSHIP LINKS & THE CARDS
 For the Cards System, the Links provide the Planetary relationship that 2 SunCards have between them, one Planet on the Pure Spread (idealistic or fantasy in mode) 
and one Planet on the Life Spread (worldly and dualistic in action) – see copies of 
these two Spreads above and the material on page 50 forward. 
 The Planetary Order always starts as 1, with the Sun, then in this order, Mercury, 
Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune, Pluto, Bacchus, Vulcan, Moon and ends
with the Earth Card being Number 13. 
 The Links between 2 Cards on the Spreads are found via four simple methods, 
and are in this order of priority: 
 1: Horizontal count, forwards or backwards, in other words, the Sun-Card 
of one is in a Planetary position of the other’s Chart. 
 In our example above, #1’s Moon was #2’s Sun on the Life Spread, so the pair 
have a worldly Moon Link. A nice relationship potential, as I noted above, as long as it 
is attraction, not repulsion. Remember that the Moon brings up stuff from old patterns 
and deeply ingrained habits that often will lock us into repetitive behaviors until they can 
be recognized and dealt with. They also have a Vulcan Horizontal Link on the Pure 
Spread, this is about connections that are really here to help, or be helped by, the 
other, a Soul Help-mate. Conversely, if this becomes a negative connection, there will 
be duplicity and/or restrictions, a Soul Destroyer. If no Horizontal Link is found then 
we move to the next step. 
 2: The Vertical Link uses the columns for a connection. Note the ⇐ / ⇒’s 
at the top of each Spread indicate that the two outer Cards of the three on top can move 
to take any of the places in the remaining Columns - K♠ to the left and J♠/10♣ to 
 
22
the right. This allows all columns to have an equal number of Cards which provides 
consistency throughout. 
 The count starts from the Card closest to the first or Mercury Row and proceeds 
downward in the standard Planetary order as before, the three Cards placed at the top 
are in fact the last Row by following the logic of the Pure Spread (see my title The “Key” 
Spread AC: 8 for further clarification). [Richmond’s design for these in the original 
needed them on top to give space for his Titles and to fit his rising sun motif above 
the whole thing – see last section page 45 and on.] Once you have found one on the 
Vertical, stop; if not, then we go to the next step. 
 For an example I will use the 7♣ and 9♠ on the Pure Spread where we find a 
Jupiter Vertical. The 7♣ = Sun is nearest the top, count downwards, A♦ = Mercury, 8♦ = 
Venus, 2♠ = Mars, and 9♠ = Jupiter. On the Life Spread we also have a Vertical Link 
between these two, but this time it is Mars. The 9♠ is the nearest to the top, so count 
downwards, J♠ = Mercury, A♥ = Venus and 7♣ = Mars. 
 Remember, the Top Row Cards, and their movability, are only to be used for 
those who wish to read in this Vertical Direction as initiated by Lein and Camp. 
 3. The Diagonal Link is just as it sounds, find one of the Cards in question 
then trace out a line on any of the available diagonal lines. If the other Card is on one 
of those lines, then count the Planetary order sequence between them, in the same order, 
as noted above. If there is no Diagonal, then on to the final method, as follows. 
 4. The Rectangular Link is a little more complicated to work out but if 
you do not have a Planetary Link by any of the previous three methods, then this is 
the answer and is surprisingly simple once you have understood the method. 
 For an example, I will use the 5♣ and the 5♦ (dark-shaded on the portion of 
the Life Spread illustrated below) as the pair we are looking for. If you check the Life 
Spread you will find no Horizontal, no Vertical, and no Diagonal Links whatsoever; this 
is where the Rectangular Link concept came in. The method is quite simple, every part 
 
23
of the count is done in two straight lines, one direction followed by one at right-angles to 
it, like a Horizontal then a Vertical, in either a Backward or Forward, or an Up or Down 
direction. As always, start the count from one Sun-Card going as far as is required then 
to complete the count, a right-angled turn is made and one continues on to the Card in 
question. So to use our example, starting from the 5♣ as Sun and going forward (following the light shaded squares). Horizontally, the 3♦ is Mercury, A♠ Venus, turn rightangles downward to 2♥ Mars, 8♣ Jupiter, A♦ Saturn and finishing with 5♦ as a Uranus 
Link for the two. This can go the other way, too, starting with the 5♦ as Sun and going 
backward (following the gridded squares) Horizontally, the 7♣ is Mercury, 9♥ Venus, 
turn right-angles upward to 8♠ Mars, 2♦ Jupiter, 9♣ Saturn and finishing with 5♣ as a 
Uranus Link – same result.

RELATIONSHIP CHARTS
 This whole project stems from an Astrological technique, with two variant methods, 
whereby a new chart is created that represents the joining of two or more people in some 
sort of partnership. The initial version takes the individual Birth Charts of the couple, 
converting the Signs into Numerical Degrees, adding the two together and then dividing 
the result by 2 – essentially a Mid-Point concept. A newer version, known as a Davidson 
Relationship Chart, works by doing the Mid-Point work not on the various Planetary 
Degrees, but starting from the Dates, Times & Locations of the couple involved and then 
creating a Chart based on that data. To help explain how to find a Mid-Point Date, one 
converts each Birthday (mm/dd/yy) to its Julian Number, add those two and divide by 2, 
getting an answer that converts back to a new day; if the division yields a Half in the 
result, then one would add 12 hours to the Time calculation. The time work is relatively 
easy, convert Birth times to a 24-Hour notation, add together and divide the result by 2; 
adding 12 hours if the Half-a-day result occurs. Then add the two Longitudes and 
Latitudes of the Birthplaces of the couple, divide those results by 2, and the final Date, 
Time and Place is used to create a new Birth Chart that represents the couple. 
 Both of these techniques are known as Composite Charts. This is an obvious misnomer as they both rely on Division not Composition to arrive at their results. Essentially, both versions show a picture of a couple meeting in the middle of every facet of 
life, which is nonsensical. In life the Venus or Moon energy will be much stronger for a 
woman, whereas the Mars or Saturn will be so for a man – most relationships fail for the 
very reason that the couples will rarely ever achieve that mid-way harmony. In fact, it is 
those off-balance energies that, when developed and harmonized in a relationship, allow 
for both his and/or her strengths to be maximized and also their weaknesses to be compensated for by the other. Then, with the Davidson method you can have the couple’s

chart based on a place high up a mountain, or way out to sea, even so, it is a much more 
logical relationship concept than flat out Mid-Points. Also, with the Davidson version, 
you get an actual Birth Chart for the two people as a unit, which also can be Progressed, 
or have Solar Returns, plus many other techniques can be applied; Arabic Parts, Degree 
Symbolism and other such methods.
